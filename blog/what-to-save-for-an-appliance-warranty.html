What to Save for an Appliance Warranty

November 7, 2026 • 6 min read

Most warranty calls fail for a boring reason. The appliance is still inside the coverage window, and nobody can find the receipt or read the serial number without pulling the unit out of a tight space. The useful moment is the day it arrives, while the box is still in the kitchen.

Manufacturers set their own rules. Some want a registered product. Some only want proof of purchase. Read the card that came in the box, then save the pieces below so you are not reconstructing them from email two years later.

Save these four things

  • The receipt or invoice, with the date and the price. A retailer order email counts. A photo of a paper receipt is fine if the whole page is readable, including the date.
  • Brand, model, and serial number. Photograph the plate before the appliance is pushed against the wall. The label scanning guide is the short version of that job.
  • The warranty term and the end date. “One year from purchase” is not a date you will calculate correctly under stress. Write the end date down.
  • Who sold it and who installed it. A store warranty, a manufacturer warranty, and a home-warranty contract are three different phone numbers.

Registering the product on the manufacturer’s site is optional for some brands and required for others. Registration is not a substitute for the receipt. Keep both.

If the receipt is already gone

Look in this order, and stop when you have a date and a seller:

  1. The store account or email search for the brand name.
  2. The card or bank statement for that month. It proves a payment, not always the exact model.
  3. The delivery or install invoice, which sometimes lists the model even when the register receipt does not.
  4. The previous owner’s folder, if you bought the house with the appliance. Note that the warranty may not transfer. The paper will say.

If you cannot find a date, record “purchase date unknown” and keep the serial number anyway. A service visit still needs the model. An unknown date is better than a date you invented.

What a claim usually asks for

Have these in one place before you call. You will not want to crawl behind the refrigerator while you are on hold.

  • Model and serial number.
  • Purchase date and proof of purchase.
  • A short description of the failure, and when it started.
  • Photos of the problem, if it is visible: a leak, an error code, a damaged part.
  • Your own notes from any earlier repair on that same unit.

Extended warranties sold at checkout are separate products. The end date on the manufacturer warranty and the end date on the store plan are often different. Write both, or you will call the wrong desk.

A reminder before it expires

A warranty you remember the week after it ends is not useful. Put a reminder a month before the end date. That month is when a noisy fridge or a dishwasher that needs a second cycle is still someone else’s problem to look at.

If the repair quote arrives after coverage ends, the next decision is whether to fix it or replace it. Age, what you paid, and what the repair costs are the inputs. The Burden Ratio guide walks through that comparison. It does not apply while a warranty will still cover the repair.

New homeowners can grab the plates for every appliance in one pass. That checklist is what to record after buying a house. The same photos also start an insurance inventory, which is a different file with a different purpose: proof of what you owned, not proof that a manufacturer still owes you a repair.
