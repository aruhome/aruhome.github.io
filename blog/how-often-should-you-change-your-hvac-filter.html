How Often Should You Change Your HVAC Filter?

August 1, 2026 • 6 min read

Changing your HVAC filter is one of the simplest ways to keep your heating and cooling system operating properly. A dirty or clogged filter restricts airflow, which can make the system run longer and harder.

So how often should you change it?

For many homes, checking the filter every month and replacing it when it is dirty is a good approach. As a general minimum, the U.S. Department of Energy recommends replacing a filter at least every three months. Some systems and households need more frequent changes.

The correct schedule depends on your HVAC system, filter type, home, and how heavily the system is used.

Why Does the HVAC Filter Need to Be Changed?

The filter catches dust, pollen, and other particles before they enter the HVAC system. As the filter becomes loaded with debris, airflow can decrease.

Reduced airflow can cause:

  • Longer heating or cooling cycles
  • Reduced comfort
  • Increased strain on the HVAC system
  • Higher energy use
  • Poorer indoor air circulation
  • Potential system performance problems

The filter also helps protect components inside the HVAC system. DOE guidance notes that a dirty filter can significantly reduce airflow and increase furnace runtime, motor wear, and energy consumption.

How Often Should You Change an HVAC Filter?

There isn't one replacement schedule that works for every home.

A practical starting point is:

Check the filter once a month. Replace it when it is visibly dirty, or according to the schedule recommended by the filter and HVAC manufacturer.

DOE consumer guidance recommends checking the filter monthly and replacing it if dirty, with a minimum replacement interval of about three months.

You may need to replace the filter more frequently if:

  • You have pets that shed heavily.
  • Someone in the home has significant allergies or sensitivities.
  • Your home becomes dusty quickly.
  • The HVAC system runs for long periods.
  • You use a smaller, thinner filter that loads quickly.
  • Your filter manufacturer recommends a shorter interval.

If you are unsure, check the HVAC manufacturer's instructions before changing the filter schedule.

What You Need

Usually, this task requires only:

  • A replacement HVAC filter
  • The correct filter size
  • A few minutes of your time

The filter size is commonly printed on the cardboard frame of the existing filter, such as 16 × 25 × 1.

Before buying a replacement, confirm the size and filter specifications required by your system.

How to Change an HVAC Filter

1. Turn off the HVAC system

Set the thermostat to off before removing the filter. This prevents the system from operating while you are working on it.

2. Locate the filter

The filter may be located:

Behind a return-air grille

Inside a filter slot near the furnace or air handler

In another location specified by your HVAC system

If you have more than one return grille, check whether each one contains a filter.

3. Remove the old filter

Carefully slide the old filter out.

Take note of the airflow arrow printed on the filter. The arrow indicates the direction air should travel through the filter.

4. Check the old filter

Look at how much dust and debris has accumulated.

This is useful for determining whether your replacement schedule is appropriate. If a filter becomes heavily loaded well before your normal replacement date, consider checking it more frequently.

5. Install the new filter

Insert the replacement filter with the airflow arrow pointing in the correct direction—normally toward the HVAC equipment.

Make sure the filter fits properly without forcing it into place.

6. Turn the HVAC system back on

Restore the thermostat to its normal setting and make sure the system operates normally.

What MERV Rating Should You Use?

MERV stands for Minimum Efficiency Reporting Value and describes how effectively an HVAC filter captures certain particles.

A higher MERV rating is not automatically better for every HVAC system.

A filter that is too restrictive for a particular system can reduce airflow. DOE guidance notes that filter selection should account for airflow and system requirements.

Rather than choosing the highest MERV rating available, follow your HVAC manufacturer's recommendation or ask a qualified HVAC professional if you're unsure.

How Do You Know If Your Filter Is Too Dirty?

Look at the filter rather than relying only on a calendar.

Signs that it may need replacement include:

  • A visible layer of dust covering the filter
  • Dust buildup around the filter
  • Reduced airflow from vents
  • The filter looks significantly darker than when new
  • The system is running longer than usual

A dirty filter does not necessarily mean the HVAC system has a problem. It may simply mean the filter needs to be replaced.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Installing the filter backward

The airflow arrow matters. Installing the filter in the wrong direction can reduce its effectiveness.

Choosing a filter without checking compatibility

Don't assume every filter with the same dimensions is appropriate for your system.

Waiting for the filter to become completely clogged

Checking the filter monthly makes it easier to catch excessive buildup before airflow becomes significantly restricted.

Automatically choosing the highest MERV rating

Higher filtration isn't necessarily appropriate for every HVAC system. Follow the equipment manufacturer's recommendations.

When Should You Call an HVAC Professional?

Changing a filter is normally a straightforward maintenance task, but contact an HVAC professional if you notice:

  • Unusual noises
  • Repeated system shutdowns
  • Poor heating or cooling after replacing the filter
  • Frozen coils
  • Burning smells
  • Significant or persistent airflow problems

A filter replacement should not be used as a substitute for diagnosing an HVAC system that isn't operating correctly.

How Often Should You Put This Task on Your Maintenance Schedule?

For most households, a useful routine is:

Check monthly → replace when dirty → follow the manufacturer's recommended maximum interval.

If your household has pets, heavy dust, or other conditions that load the filter quickly, check it more frequently.
