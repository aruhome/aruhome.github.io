How to Deep Clean a Dryer Vent

August 1, 2026 • 6 min read

Cleaning your dryer vent is an important home maintenance task that is easy to overlook.

Lint can accumulate inside the dryer vent and ductwork, restricting airflow. The U.S. Fire Administration recommends cleaning dryer lint filters before use and cleaning dryer vent ductwork every year.

A clogged or restricted vent can also make clothes take longer to dry.

How Often Should You Clean a Dryer Vent?

A good starting point is:

Clean the dryer vent ductwork at least once a year.

You may need to inspect or clean it more frequently if:

  • Clothes take longer than normal to dry
  • You frequently dry heavy loads
  • The dryer is used by a large household
  • The vent run is long
  • The duct has multiple bends
  • You notice significant lint buildup
  • Airflow at the exterior vent is weak

USFA guidance recommends annual vent cleaning and identifies longer-than-normal drying time as an important warning sign.

Why Does Dryer Vent Cleaning Matter?

Your dryer needs to move warm, moist air outside the home.

When lint or other debris restricts the vent:

  • Clothes can take longer to dry
  • The dryer may run longer
  • Heat and moisture can build up
  • The appliance may work harder
  • The vent system may become a fire-safety concern

USFA reports that failure to clean was the leading contributing factor in clothes dryer fires in its cited 2018–2020 data.

What You'll Need

For a basic accessible vent connection:

  • Vacuum with a hose
  • Dryer vent cleaning brush or appropriate duct-cleaning kit
  • Screwdriver if required for your connection
  • Flashlight
  • Replacement metal ducting if the existing duct is damaged

Use equipment appropriate for your dryer and vent configuration.

Before You Start

Turn off the dryer

Do not operate the dryer while you are cleaning the vent.

Disconnect power

Unplug an electric dryer before moving it, following the manufacturer's instructions.

If you have a gas dryer and need to disconnect or disturb the gas connection, use a qualified professional rather than attempting work you are not equipped to perform safely.

Pull the dryer away from the wall

Move it carefully so you can access the vent connection.

Avoid crushing or sharply bending the vent duct.

How to Clean a Dryer Vent

1. Clean the lint filter

Remove the lint filter and clean it thoroughly.

USFA recommends cleaning the lint filter before using the dryer.

If the filter has residue from dryer sheets or fabric softener, follow the manufacturer's cleaning instructions.

2. Inspect the area behind the dryer

Look for:

  • Excessive lint
  • Crushed ducting
  • Damaged ducting
  • Loose connections
  • Objects blocking the dryer

Keep combustible materials away from the dryer.

3. Disconnect the vent duct

Carefully disconnect the duct from the dryer according to the appliance manufacturer's instructions.

4. Remove accumulated lint

Use an appropriate dryer-vent brush or cleaning system to loosen lint inside the duct.

A vacuum can help remove loosened debris.

Work carefully so you don't damage or disconnect the duct.

5. Clean the dryer connection

Remove lint from the dryer's exhaust connection without pushing debris deeper into the appliance.

If you suspect lint has accumulated inside the dryer itself, follow the manufacturer's instructions or have the appliance professionally serviced.

6. Check the exterior vent

Go outside and inspect the dryer exhaust vent.

The exterior flap should be able to open when the dryer operates, and the vent should not be blocked.

7. Reconnect the duct

Reconnect the vent securely.

The duct should not be crushed behind the dryer.

8. Test the dryer

Run the dryer according to the manufacturer's instructions and check that air is exhausting properly from the exterior vent.

Warning Signs of a Restricted Dryer Vent

Pay attention to:

  • Clothes taking much longer to dry
  • Clothes remaining damp after a normal cycle
  • The dryer becoming unusually hot
  • Weak airflow from the outside vent
  • Excessive lint around the dryer
  • A burning smell
  • The dryer repeatedly shutting down

If you notice a burning smell, smoke, unusual overheating, or other immediate safety concerns, stop using the dryer and have it inspected.

Dryer Vent Materials Matter

Follow your dryer's installation instructions and local requirements.

USFA educational material recommends proper venting and warns against materials such as plastic or foil venting that can melt or burn.

If you discover damaged, inappropriate, or poorly installed ductwork, consider having it replaced by a qualified professional.

When Should You Call a Professional?

Consider professional service if:

  • The vent runs a long distance
  • The duct is inaccessible
  • You cannot safely disconnect the dryer
  • The vent is heavily clogged
  • You find damaged or improper ducting
  • The dryer still takes too long to dry after cleaning
  • You have a gas dryer and need gas-line work
  • You suspect lint has accumulated inside the appliance

Don't take apart gas or electrical components unless you are qualified to do so.

Dryer Maintenance Checklist

Every time you use the dryer:

  • Clean the lint filter

    • Periodically:

    • Check the exterior vent
    • Check drying performance
    • Inspect the duct for crushing or damage

    At least annually:

    • Clean the dryer vent ductwork
    • Inspect the vent connection
    • Record the maintenance date
