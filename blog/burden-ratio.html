The Homeowner’s "Fix or Flop" Guide: Understanding the AruHome Burden Ratio™

August 15, 2026 • 6 min read

When your water heater breaks for the third time in two years, should you repair it or replace it? Most homeowners guess—and guess wrong.

Homeownership is full of difficult financial decisions, but perhaps none is as frustrating as an aging appliance that keeps demanding repairs. You've already spent $200 fixing the dishwasher pump last year, and now the control board is dead. The repairman quotes you $350. Do you throw more money at it, or is it time to cut your losses and buy new?

Defining the AruHome Burden Ratio™

To stop making emotional decisions about your appliances, you need to rely on the math. Enter the AruHome Burden Ratio™. The AruHome Burden Ratio™ is a practical decision-making metric that weighs the cost of a repair against the depreciated value of the asset and its age. As an appliance ages, its value drops while the probability of future breakdowns increases.

The 50% Guideline

A practical rule of thumb is the 50% Guideline. If a repair costs more than 50% of the value of a comparable new unit (factoring in the age of your current unit), it’s often time to move on.

For example, if a new refrigerator costs $1,000, and your current 8-year-old fridge needs a $400 compressor repair, your AruHome Burden Ratio™ is dangerously high. While $400 is less than $1,000, you are investing 40% of the replacement cost into a machine that is already near the end of its typical 10-12 year lifespan.

Hidden Costs of Old Appliances

When calculating whether to repair or replace, you must also consider the hidden costs: Energy Efficiency. An air conditioning unit from 2012 operates far less efficiently than a modern model. By opting to repair a dying, inefficient unit, you are not only paying the repair bill but continuing to pay a premium on your monthly utility bills.
