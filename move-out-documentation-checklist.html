Move-Out Documentation Checklist

Photograph the empty apartment the same way you did on move-in day. The comparison, not a pile of unrelated pictures, is what settles a deposit dispute.

1. Match the move-in photos

  • Open the move-in checklist notes and retake each room from the same doorway.
  • Photograph the same scuffs you recorded on day one, so it is clear they were already there.
  • Shoot walls, floors, windows, and the inside of closets after the furniture is gone.
  • If you never made move-in photos, still shoot every empty room. Say so in the notes instead of implying you have a before picture.

2. Cleaning and the appliances you used

  • Photograph the oven inside and out, the stovetop, and the inside of the refrigerator and freezer.
  • Photograph the tub, sink, toilet, and the floor around them after they are clean.
  • Run the disposal once, then photograph under the sink so a drip is visible if there is one.
  • Replace any bulbs you removed. A dark fixture looks like damage in a walkthrough.

3. Repairs you made

  • Photograph patched nail holes, filled chips, and any spot you painted.
  • Keep the receipt if you hired someone. A photo of the finished wall without the receipt is weaker.
  • Do not patch over water stains or anything that looks structural. Tell the landlord in writing and keep that message.

4. Keys, meters, and the walkthrough

  • Count keys, fobs, and remotes against what you received. Photograph the set you are returning.
  • Read the utility meters if the account is in your name, and photograph the reading.
  • Ask for a walkthrough. Write down what was said, who was there, and the date.
  • Export one PDF of the photos and notes, and keep a copy outside the apartment.

Belongings you own, as opposed to the unit itself, belong on the renter's home inventory checklist.
