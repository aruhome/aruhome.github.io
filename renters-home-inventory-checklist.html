Renter's Home Inventory Checklist

A renter's policy covers what you own, not the building. The useful list is the one you can open after a fire or a break-in: the item, the room, what you paid, and a photo. Your policy decides what is actually paid. This checklist is the paperwork people are usually asked to produce.

1. Write this down for each item

  • What it is, and which room it lives in.
  • Brand, model, and serial number when the item has a plate or a label.
  • Purchase date and price, or your best estimate if the receipt is gone. Label a guess as a guess.
  • A photo of the item in the apartment, plus a photo of the receipt if you still have it.

2. Electronics and furniture

  • Televisions, laptops, phones, cameras, consoles, and audio equipment.
  • Beds, sofas, dressers, desks, and a dining set you paid for. Everyday dishes do not each need a line.
  • Photograph the tag or the serial label before the item is against the wall.
  • Bicycles, instruments, and tools in a closet or on a balcony are easy to forget. Add them.

3. What is yours, and what is the landlord's

  • The building's refrigerator, range, and heater are the landlord's. Photograph their condition on the move-in checklist, and do not list them as your property.
  • Your microwave, your couch, and your laptop are yours.
  • Jewelry, art, and collections often need a separate note or appraisal. Keep that paper with the photo.
  • Skip groceries and toiletries. They crowd out the items you would actually replace.

4. A copy that survives the apartment

  • Export a PDF when the first pass is done.
  • Keep that copy somewhere that is not only the phone sitting in the apartment.
  • Add a line when you buy something expensive. A yearly rewrite is how lists go stale.

The longer version of this list, including whole-house systems, is the home inventory list for insurance.
