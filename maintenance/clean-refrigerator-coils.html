How to Clean Refrigerator Coils

Cleaning your refrigerator coils is a simple maintenance task that takes about 30 minutes. By removing dust and debris, you help your fridge run more efficiently, save money on energy bills, and prevent costly breakdowns.

Time Required 30 mins
Difficulty Easy
DIY Skill Level 1

Why It Matters

Dirty coils can increase energy usage by up to 30%. Dust and pet hair restrict airflow, forcing the compressor to work harder and dramatically shortening its lifespan.

How Often?

Every 6 months for most homes. If you have shedding pets or live in a dusty environment, check and clean them every 3 to 4 months.

What You'll Need

  • Vacuum with brush or crevice attachment
  • Coil cleaning brush (long, flexible bristles)
  • Flashlight
  • Dust mask (optional)

Before You Start

  • Safely move the refrigerator by gently pulling it straight out to avoid scratching the floor.
  • Locate your coils: they are usually behind a bottom toe kick grille or exposed on the back of older units.
  • Turn off power or unplug the unit to avoid electrical shock.

Step-by-Step Guide

1

Unplug the Unit

Carefully pull the refrigerator away from the wall and unplug it from the electrical outlet.

2

Vacuum the Coils

Locate the condenser coils (usually behind a base grille at the bottom or on the back). Use a vacuum with a brush attachment to remove the top layer of loose dust and pet hair.

3

Brush Off Stubborn Debris

Use a specialized coil brush to gently scrape and loosen stubborn dirt from between the coils. Vacuum the dislodged dirt immediately.

Signs It Needs Attention

  • The refrigerator feels warm or isn't cooling properly.
  • The compressor runs constantly without cycling off.
  • Your energy bills are unusually high.
  • The exterior of the fridge feels very warm to the touch.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Using a stiff wire brush: Scraping too hard can bend the delicate cooling fins. Always use a soft, flexible coil brush.

Spraying liquids: Never spray water or liquid cleaners on the coils, as this can damage electrical components.

When to Call a Professional

You should call an appliance repair technician if:

  • You've cleaned the coils but the fridge still isn't cooling properly.
  • The compressor makes loud, unusual noises.
  • You spot a leak or smell burning odors near the compressor area.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should refrigerator coils be cleaned?

Typically, you should clean them every 6 months. However, if you have pets that shed heavily or live in a dusty environment, it's best to clean them every 3-4 months.

Can dirty coils make a refrigerator stop cooling?

Yes. When coils are caked with dust, they cannot effectively release heat. This causes the compressor to overheat and shut down, leading to a fridge that fails to cool.

Can I vacuum refrigerator coils?

Yes, vacuuming is the primary way to clean them. Use a crevice tool or brush attachment to suck up loose dust, then use a coil brush to loosen stubborn dirt and vacuum that up as well.
