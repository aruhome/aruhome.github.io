New Homeowner Toolkit

First-week checklist for a new home

A short list for the first day and the first week. The full guide, including the first month and the first 90 days, is at aruhome.github.io/new-homeowner-toolkit/.

A burning smell, a live leak, a gas odor, or a spark is not a task to finish later. Leave the area and call emergency services or the utility from a safe place. For a suspected gas leak, do not flip switches or use a phone in the area.

First 24 hours

  1. Locate the main water shutoff valve.Write the location. Turn it only enough to learn the direction, unless a leak is active.
  2. Locate the electrical panel and read the circuit labels.Do not remove the cover. If a breaker trips again after one reset, call a qualified electrician.
  3. Locate the thermostat and the heating and cooling equipment.Note furnace, heat pump, boiler, or window units. Leave combustion equipment to a technician.
  4. Confirm smoke alarms and carbon monoxide alarms, and test them.Use the test button in the instructions. Missing alarms, or a failed test, need attention before the first night if you can arrange it.
  5. Walk the exits and agree on a meeting place.Include a way out of sleeping rooms.
  6. Check door locks, window locks, and garage access.If the previous owner still has keys or a code, rekey or reprogram as soon as you reasonably can.
  7. Confirm water, power, heat or cooling, and internet or phone service are on.Write the utility name. Keep account numbers on the bill, not on this sheet if you will hand it to someone else.
  8. Gather manuals, keys, remotes, and any folder the seller left.Put them in one place so they are not packed with the dishes.
  9. List urgent issues from the inspection or final walkthrough.Separate “call someone now” from “get an estimate this month.”

First week

  1. Save the home inspection report with the closing file.Keep a second copy outside the house.
  2. File the closing documents and the deed or condo ownership papers.Ask the closing agent which copies to keep. This sheet does not interpret those documents.
  3. Photograph model and serial plates on major appliances.Refrigerator, range, dishwasher, washer, dryer, water heater, and HVAC equipment.
  4. Record the HVAC filter size and what the manual says about replacement.Use the size printed on the old filter.
  5. Find fixture shutoffs and any accessible cleanout.Look under sinks and behind the toilet. If a valve is stuck, do not force it.
  6. Record the water heater model, fuel type, and age if you can see it.When the age code is unclear, write “unknown.”
  7. Put warranties and any home warranty or builder packet in one place.Read the claim steps and the deadlines. Coverage is whatever the contract says.
  8. List keys, garage remotes, and who has a copy.Keep codes in a password manager or a locked note. Do not write codes on a sheet you will email.
  9. Write the known repairs and who was supposed to finish them.Include seller credits or work the contract still requires, and the date you were told.
  10. Ask for any maintenance history the seller or builder can share.Roof age, HVAC service, and water-heater replacement are the notes people wish they had later.
  11. Photograph existing damage before you paint or move furniture against it.Walls, floors, windows, and stains.
  12. List recurring services: trash, water, power, gas, and any subscription.Names and phone numbers are enough on a shared copy.
  13. If there is an HOA or condo association, get the rules that assign maintenance.Who maintains the roof, the boiler, the windows, and the driveway may not be the unit owner.
